## What is IVF

In Vitro Fertilisation (IVF): an egg and sperm are combined outside the body ("in vitro" —
in glass/lab) rather than inside the uterus. Falls under Assisted Reproductive Technology
(ART) — medical procedures primarily addressing infertility.

Key terms:
- **Oocyte** — immature female egg cell (ovum), collected from the ovary; can be cryopreserved.
- **Zygote** — the fertilised egg.
- **Embryo culture** — growing the resultant embryo in an artificial lab medium for 2–6 days
  before transfer.
- IVF can succeed via egg donation for women who are post-menopausal, have infertile
  partners, or have idiopathic female-fertility issues.

## The IVF Process (7 stages, ~3–6 weeks per cycle)

1. **Preparation** — medical history review, fertility testing/investigations for both
   partners; birth control pills or estrogen may be used to control cycle timing.
2. **Ovarian Stimulation** — hormonal injections stimulate development of multiple mature
   eggs (vs. the single egg per natural cycle); monitored via scans/blood tests; a
   **trigger injection** is given ~36 hours before egg retrieval to complete maturation.
3. **Egg Retrieval** — mature eggs collected transvaginally with a thin needle under
   sedation; placed in culture medium and incubated.
4. **Fertilization** — eggs fertilised with sperm in the lab; **ICSI** (Intracytoplasmic
   Sperm Injection — a single sperm injected directly into the egg) may be used.
   ~70% of mature eggs fertilise on average.
5. **Embryo Development** — fertilised eggs are monitored as they divide; ~50% of
   fertilised embryos reach the **blastocyst stage** (suitable for transfer or freezing).
6. **Embryo Transfer** — a selected embryo is placed into the uterus via a thin catheter
   through the cervix (similar to a pelvic exam). Possible after-effects: mild cramping,
   bloating, breast tenderness, spotting, constipation.
7. **Pregnancy Confirmation** — blood pregnancy test ~14 days post-transfer. If negative
   and frozen embryos remain, a Frozen Embryo Transfer (FET) may be tried next; otherwise
   another full IVF cycle may be needed.

**Key takeaways:**
- Flow: stimulation → egg retrieval → fertilization → embryo development → embryo
  transfer → pregnancy testing.
- Attrition happens at every stage — eggs retrieved ≠ embryos available for transfer.
- Age at egg retrieval strongly influences success rates.
- Extra suitable embryos can be frozen for future transfer attempts.

## Pre-IVF Testing

Purpose: understand both partners' reproductive health, flag factors that could affect
fertilization/pregnancy, and personalize the IVF treatment plan. Not every test applies to
every patient — there's a **core panel** (always/almost always done) and **indicated-only**
tests (done selectively).

### Core panel

| Test | For | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Medical & Fertility History | Both | Prior conception attempts, medical conditions, fertility history — identifies risk factors, guides further eval |
| AMH (Anti-Müllerian Hormone) | Female | Ovarian reserve — informs stimulation planning |
| FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone) | Female | Ovarian reserve / reproductive hormone function |
| LH (Luteinizing Hormone) | Female | Hormonal balance / ovulatory function |
| Prolactin | Female | Hormonal abnormalities affecting reproduction |
| Thyroid Function Tests | Both | Thyroid abnormalities affecting fertility/pregnancy |
| Transvaginal Ultrasound | Female | Ovarian structure, follicle count, uterine anatomy, endometrium, fibroids, cysts |
| Semen Analysis | Male | Sperm count, motility, morphology |
| General Health Screening | Both | Overall treatment readiness |
| HIV Screening | Both | Infection screening / treatment safety |
| Hepatitis B & C Testing | Both | Infection screening / pregnancy considerations |
| Syphilis Testing (VDRL/RPR) | Both | Infection screening |

### Indicated-only tests

| Test | For | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hysteroscopy / Saline Scan | Female | Pre-IVF or before embryo transfer | Detailed uterine cavity evaluation — implantation-affecting abnormalities |
| Male Hormonal Profile | Male | When male infertility suspected | Hormonal causes of low sperm production |
| Genetic Carrier Screening | Both | When indicated | Inherited conditions that could affect offspring |
| Hemoglobin Electrophoresis (HbA/HbA2/HbF by HPLC) | Both, when indicated | Pre-IVF or pre-pregnancy | Thalassemia/hemoglobinopathy carrier screening — a specific form of genetic carrier screening; identifies raised HbA2/HbF fractions suggestive of thalassemia trait, informing offspring risk if both partners carry a trait |
| Karyotyping | Both | When indicated | Chromosomal abnormalities — relevant in recurrent IVF failure |
| Endometrial Receptivity Assessment | Female | Before embryo transfer | Timing of uterine lining readiness for implantation |

### The four questions pre-IVF evaluation answers

1. How is ovarian reserve and function? (AMH, FSH, LH, ultrasound/follicle count)
2. Is there a male-factor issue? (semen analysis ± hormonal profile)
3. Is the uterus suitable for implantation? (ultrasound ± hysteroscopy/saline scan)
4. Are there hormonal/infectious/genetic/general-health factors to consider? (blood tests,
   selected advanced investigations)

Findings from all of the above feed into the fertility team's decisions on: IVF strategy,
stimulation protocol, fertilization method (e.g. standard IVF vs. ICSI), and whether more
workup/treatment is needed before proceeding.

## Pregnancy / Antenatal Monitoring (added in a later revision of the source doc)

The source doc now extends past "Pre-IVF Testing" into what happens **after** a pregnancy is
confirmed — this section was added specifically to cover the report types the client actually
sent (see [[sample-reports-analysis]]), which skewed toward prenatal/pregnancy monitoring
rather than the pre-IVF panel above.

Framed as a practical **IVF/pregnancy report-summary framework**, not a rigid clinical
protocol — timing varies by clinic, maternal risk, and singleton vs. multiple pregnancy.

### Antenatal test/scan table (selected, roughly in chronological order)

| Test / Evaluation | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy Confirmation (β-hCG) | Early pregnancy, after transfer or missed period | Confirm biochemical pregnancy; serial values track early progression |
| Early Pregnancy / Viability Ultrasound | ~6–8 weeks | Confirm intrauterine location, early viability, number of pregnancies |
| Blood Pressure Monitoring | Throughout, every visit | Hypertension / preeclampsia trend monitoring |
| Urine Examination | Throughout, routine visits | Proteinuria, UTI signs, preeclampsia context |
| CBC / Hemoglobin | Initial + repeated later (esp. 3rd trimester) | Detects anemia, blood-cell status |
| Blood Group & Rh Typing | Early pregnancy | Rh-negative management planning |
| Infectious Disease Screening (HIV, Hep B, Syphilis) | Early pregnancy | Maternal/fetal risk, management |
| Thyroid Function Tests | Early pregnancy / when indicated | Thyroid abnormalities affecting pregnancy |
| Blood Glucose / Diabetes Screening | Early if risk factors; routine ~24–28 weeks | Gestational diabetes |
| First-Trimester / Dating Ultrasound | ~11–14 weeks | Confirms gestational age, fetus count, early anatomy |
| Nuchal Translucency (NT) / Combined Screening | ~11–14 weeks | Combines USG + serum markers → Down/Edward/Patau risk |
| NIPT / Cell-Free DNA Screening | From early pregnancy | cfDNA-based aneuploidy screening — **screening, not diagnostic** |
| Second-Trimester Serum / Quad Test | ~14–20 weeks (if first-trimester combined wasn't done) | Alternate chromosomal/fetal condition risk estimate |
| Anomaly / Detailed Anatomy Scan | ~18–21 weeks | Detailed fetal structure/growth/placenta evaluation |
| Fetal Echocardiography | ~20–22 weeks, if indicated | Detailed fetal cardiac structure/function |
| Placental Assessment | Mid-pregnancy onward, on USG | Placenta location/appearance (e.g. previa) |
| Fetal Growth Ultrasound | Later pregnancy, risk-based frequency | Growth trajectory, amniotic fluid |
| Fundal Height / Abdominal Growth | Mid-pregnancy onward, routine visits | Simple longitudinal growth indicator |
| Fetal Movement Assessment | Later pregnancy | Perceived fetal activity changes |
| Doppler Studies | Later/high-risk pregnancy | Placental/fetal blood flow, growth restriction workup |
| Non-Stress Test (NST) | Later/high-risk pregnancy | Fetal heart-rate response / well-being |
| Biophysical Profile (BPP) | Later/high-risk pregnancy | Combines movement, tone, breathing, fluid, NST |
| Repeat CBC / Maternal Blood Tests | ~3rd trimester | Reassess anemia/blood status |
| Group B Streptococcus (GBS) Screening | ~35–37 weeks | Informs intrapartum management |
| Fetal Presentation/Position | ~36 weeks onward | Delivery planning (head-down/breech/etc.) |
| Amniotic Fluid Assessment | Later-pregnancy USG / when indicated | Low/high fluid flag |
| Late-Pregnancy Well-being Assessment | 3rd trimester | Growth, fluid, placenta, position for delivery planning |

### Priority fields for an IVF-specific pregnancy-report summary

- **Pregnancy status:** gestational age, EDD, singleton/twins/multiple, IVF/FET cycle
  details if available, embryo transferred if relevant
- **Maternal monitoring:** BP trends, weight, Hemoglobin/CBC, blood group/Rh, glucose/OGTT,
  thyroid values, urine/protein findings
- **Fetal monitoring:** CRL/gestational measurements, fetal heart rate, NT, anatomy/anomaly
  findings, estimated fetal weight, growth percentile/trend, amniotic fluid, placental
  location, fetal presentation, Doppler findings
- **Screening:** NIPT/combined screening, genetic screening results, infection screening,
  GBS status, any positive/abnormal screening requiring follow-up
- **High-value clinical flags:** hypertension/preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, fetal
  growth restriction, abnormal Doppler, placental abnormalities, abnormal fetal anatomy,
  multiple-pregnancy complications, anything marked abnormal/positive/high-risk/needs
  follow-up

Because antenatal care is **longitudinal**, the source doc explicitly calls out that trends
across visits (growth, BP, urine protein, fetal heart rate, GA) are more valuable than any
single isolated value — implies the summarizer should ideally track/compare values across a
patient's multiple reports over time, not just summarize one report in isolation.
